# Governance

Climb and Maintain ACARS begins with a benevolent-maintainer model.

## Stewardship

Climb and Maintain, SPC is the original author and initial steward. The steward sets release scope, appoints maintainers, protects the project's license and identity, and makes final decisions when consensus cannot be reached.

## Maintainers

Maintainers may triage issues, review and merge changes, manage releases, moderate project spaces, and update policy within their granted access. They are expected to disclose relevant conflicts, preserve attribution, enforce the security and third-party IP rules, and give accessibility and flight-data durability appropriate weight.

## Decisions

Routine decisions happen through issues and pull requests. Material changes to architecture, licensing, telemetry privacy, native-library trust, supported backends, or release criteria should include a written rationale and a reasonable review period. Consensus is preferred; the steward resolves deadlock.

## Releases

A maintainer may not declare a release complete while required checks are failing or warnings remain. In particular, a public release requires machine-readable evidence that the user-supplied native-library workflow passed independently on MSFS 2020 and MSFS 2024. Simulator binary redistribution research is not a substitute for that evidence, because the project does not bundle or download those binaries.

## Contributors, attribution, and forks

Contributors retain attribution. No contributor license agreement is currently required. Forks are expressly welcome under Apache-2.0 and may use their own visible branding, while preserving required license notices and the historical statement that Climb and Maintain, SPC originally created the project.

Governance may evolve as the maintainer group grows. Changes will be documented in this file.
